How should the Integrations page be structured?

Pick the layout that feels right for navigating ~20 integrations across 6 categories

A

Category sections — scrollable single page

All integrations on one page, grouped under bold section headings (SEO Data, Analytics, Advertising, CRM, Communication, Developer). Each integration is a card with logo, name, status badge, and a Connect button. Scroll down to see all.

── SEO Data ─────────────
[Google Search Console ✓] [PageSpeed Insights ○] […]

── Analytics ────────────
[Google Analytics 4 ✓] [Mixpanel ○] [Amplitude ○]

── Advertising ──────────
[Google Ads ○] [Meta Ads ○] [LinkedIn Ads ○]

── Communication ────────
[Slack ✓] [Linear ○]

Pros

  • No navigation needed
  • Easy to scan all at once
  • Simple to build

Cons

  • Gets long as integrations grow
B

Left sidebar category filter + card grid

Sidebar on the left lists categories (All, SEO Data, Analytics, Advertising, CRM, Communication, Developer). Clicking a category filters the card grid on the right. Active category highlighted.

[All •] | [GSC ✓] [PSI ○] [GA4 ✓]
[SEO Data] | [Google Ads ○] [Meta ○]
[Analytics] | [Slack ✓] [Linear ○]
[Advertising •] |
[CRM] |
[Communication] |

Pros

  • Fast to filter by category
  • Scales well to 50+ integrations

Cons

  • More complex UI
  • Sidebar takes space
C

Tab strip by category + card grid

Horizontal tab strip at the top: All | SEO Data | Analytics | Advertising | CRM | Communication | Developer. Selected tab shows matching cards in a grid below.

[All] [SEO Data] [Analytics] [Advertising ●] [CRM] [Comm.]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Google Ads ○] [Meta Ads ○] [LinkedIn Ads ○]
[TikTok Ads ○] [Pinterest ○]

Pros

  • Clean, compact header
  • Familiar UX pattern

Cons

  • Tab strip wraps on small screens
  • Can't see categories at a glance
